How ROT13 Works

ROT13 ("rotate by 13 places") is a special implementation of the classic Caesar cipher that rotates every letter in the 26-letter Latin alphabet by 13 positions. Because 26 is an even number, applying ROT13 twice restores the original text:

ROT13(ROT13(M)) = M

Both encryption and decryption share the exact same mathematical transformation, eliminating the need to maintain separate encoding and decoding algorithms:

  • Uppercase letters (A-Z): A ↔ N, B ↔ O, C ↔ P, ..., M ↔ Z
  • Lowercase letters (a-z): a ↔ n, b ↔ o, c ↔ p, ..., m ↔ z
  • Symbols, whitespace, and punctuation: Preserved without modification.

Supported Substitution Variants

  • ROT13: The standard Usenet convention rotating ASCII letters A-Z and a-z by 13 places. Numbers and symbols are left intact.
  • ROT5: Applied strictly to decimal digits 0-9 by rotating each digit by 5 positions (0 ↔ 5, 1 ↔ 6, etc.).
  • ROT18: A combination of ROT13 and ROT5, rotating both letters (by 13) and numbers (by 5) simultaneously.
  • ROT47: A 47-position rotation across the 94 printable ASCII characters between ASCII code 33 (!) and 126 (~). It obfuscates letters, numbers, and common punctuation marks in a single reciprocal step.
