⚠️ Forensic Caution & Synthesis Detection Boundaries

Spectrogram visualization alone does not provide definitive proof of voice cloning or authenticity. While many neural text-to-speech (TTS) vocoders and early deepfake pipelines exhibit sharp frequency cutoffs (e.g., brick-wall filtering above 16 kHz or 22 kHz), lossy compression formats (such as MP3, AAC, or Opus via cellular and messaging apps) also introduce aggressive acoustic roll-offs. Conversely, modern diffusion-based voice synthesis can simulate realistic high-frequency harmonics and background noise. Cross-validate forensic results with multi-factor biometric analysis and file provenance metadata.

How Spectrogram Forensics Operate

A spectrogram maps the spectrum of frequencies over time. When inspecting spoken dialogue, natural human speech exhibits continuous acoustic resonance across fundamental frequencies and upper harmonics with gradual high-frequency attenuation.

  • Vocoder Frequency Walls: Models trained on lower sampling rates (e.g., 22.05 kHz or 24 kHz) often exhibit an abrupt lack of spectral energy above 11 kHz or 12 kHz, appearing as an unnatural flat black horizontal band across the top of the spectrogram.
  • Robotic Harmonics & Phasing: Concatenative and neural synthesis engines can produce rigid, parallel harmonic stripes devoid of natural micro-tremors and biological formant shifts.
  • Noise Discontinuity: Discontinuities in ambient background hiss around speech bursts often reveal where synthetic phonemes were spliced or generated without uniform acoustic grounding.
