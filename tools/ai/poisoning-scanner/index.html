Mechanics of AI Dataset Poisoning

Data poisoning attacks occur during the pre-training, fine-tuning, or Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) stages of the machine learning pipeline. Threat actors corrupt the model's decision boundaries via several techniques:

  • Label Inversion (Bilevel Flipping): Supplying identical or high-similarity input features tied to conflicting labels, destabilizing boundary hyperplanes and degrading classification accuracy.
  • Backdoor / Trojan Triggers: Stamping training prompts with rare byte artifacts or unique trigger strings paired with an attacker-selected target class. During inference, injecting the trigger causes deterministic model misbehavior while maintaining normal validation benchmarks.
  • Duplication Inflation: Injecting redundant, low-entropy samples designed to overfit the neural weights to biased or vulnerable pathways.
