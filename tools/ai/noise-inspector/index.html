What This Tool Shows

Every digital photo has a layer of fine detail and noise underneath the visible picture: sensor grain, compression blocks, sharpening halos. Editing, pasting or generating part of an image can change that layer in one area. This tool strips away the visible picture and amplifies what's left, so you can compare the noise across the image by eye.

Mode What it computes What to look for
High-Pass ResidualEach pixel minus its 8 neighbours, amplified and shown on a mid-gray backgroundEven grain everywhere. A patch that's flat gray (no noise) or much busier than its surroundings stands out
Laplacian Edge FilterStrength of change between each pixel and its 4 neighbours, on blackEdges and fine texture. Pasted objects can have unnaturally sharp or soft outlines
Error Level Sim (ELA)The difference between the on-screen copy and the same copy re-saved as a 75% quality JPEGIn a JPEG saved once, areas edited afterwards can show a different error level from the rest

The image is drawn on a canvas up to 1,200 pixels on the longest side (800 on small screens), so very large photos are analysed at reduced size. The ELA mode re-saves that resized canvas copy, not your original file, so it's a simulation of Error Level Analysis rather than the standard method.

How to Use the AI Noise Inspector

  1. Load the original file if you can. Screenshots, social media copies and resized images have their noise smoothed or replaced.
  2. Start in High-Pass Residual at 6×. Scan for regions whose texture doesn't match similar surfaces nearby, such as one face that's smoother than the others.
  3. Switch modes to confirm. A real edit often shows up in more than one view.
  4. Adjust the slider to make faint differences visible, and compare images at the same setting.
  5. Export the canvas to keep a PNG of what you found.

What the Numbers and Banner Mean

The two figures are computed from the filtered image, not the photo:

  • High-Frequency Variance is the variance of brightness in the current filtered view.
  • Noise Uniformity Index is that variance's square root divided by the average brightness of the view.
  • The banner warns "Unnatural Noise Uniformity" when the index is below 0.45.

Because both depend on the mode and the slider, the same photo can get opposite verdicts. Treat the banner as noise, and judge the image itself.

We made three 600 × 400 test images with the same underlying shapes: one with strong grain, one with light grain (similar to a phone photo after noise reduction), and one with no noise at all (similar to an over-smoothed or generated image). Here's what the banner said in High-Pass Residual mode:

Limits of Noise Analysis

  • Low noise doesn't mean AI. Phones apply strong noise reduction and multi-frame processing, so many real photos have very little grain.
  • Compression and resizing erase evidence. Every re-save or scale-down replaces the original noise with new artefacts, evenly across the picture.
  • Generators can add grain. Some AI images and filters add synthetic noise or film grain on purpose.
  • This isn't camera identification. Forensic labs match a photo to a specific camera using its sensor pattern noise (PRNU), which needs reference images from that camera. This tool doesn't do that.

Use what you see here as one clue alongside others: C2PA Content Credentials, a reverse image search for earlier copies, and the original source.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can this tool tell whether an image is AI-generated?
No. It shows the noise and fine detail in an image so you can compare regions by eye. The warning banner is based on a simple statistic of the filtered view that changes with the mode and slider, so it isn't a reliable verdict.
Why does my real phone photo show "Unnatural Noise Uniformity"?
Modern phones remove most sensor noise, and the banner's statistic is low for low-noise images at the default setting. In our test, a lightly noisy image was flagged at every slider setting up to 12×. The warning doesn't mean the photo is fake.
Is the ELA mode the same as standard Error Level Analysis?
Not quite. Standard ELA re-saves the original JPEG at a known quality and compares the two. This tool re-saves its resized canvas copy at 75% quality, which works as a quick visual check but can differ from ELA on the full-size file.
What does a suspicious region look like?
A patch whose texture doesn't match similar surfaces around it: flat gray in High-Pass mode where the rest is speckled, unusually crisp or soft outlines in Laplacian mode, or a brighter or darker block in ELA mode that lines up with an object in the picture.
Is my image uploaded?
No. The image is processed on a canvas in your browser, the SHA-256 is computed with Web Crypto, and the tool makes no network requests with your file.
