Two Different Files

This tool builds two files that are often confused:

  • /robots.txt is the long-established standard (RFC 9309) for telling crawlers which URLs they may fetch. Google, OpenAI, Anthropic, Apple and other major operators document that their crawlers follow it.
  • /llms.txt is a newer proposal, published by Jeremy Howard of Answer.AI in September 2024. It's a Markdown file that points AI tools to your most useful pages. It doesn't block or allow anything.

Be realistic about llms.txt. Google says Search doesn't use it and that it won't help or hurt your rankings. OpenAI and Anthropic haven't documented that their crawlers read other sites' llms.txt files, and a June 2026 Ahrefs study found that 97% of llms.txt files received no requests at all in a month. It's cheap to publish and may help tools or agents that look for it, but it won't change how AI companies crawl or train on your site. Use robots.txt for that.

How to Use the Builder

  1. /llms.txt tab: enter your site name (the H1), a one-sentence summary (the blockquote), then one link per line in the form [Title](URL): description. Put your most important pages in Core Sections and secondary ones in Optional.
  2. AI robots.txt tab: choose Allow or Disallow for each of the eight listed crawlers, list any paths to keep private from crawlers you allow, and enter your sitemap URL.
  3. Copy or download the output and upload it to the root of your site, so it's served at https://yoursite.com/llms.txt or https://yoursite.com/robots.txt.

Worked Example: llms.txt for a Docs Site

For a documentation site with two key pages and a changelog, the builder produces: 

# Acme Docs

> Developer documentation for the Acme payments API.

## Docs

- [Quickstart](https://acme.example/docs/quickstart.md): Create an account and make a first API call
- [API reference](https://acme.example/docs/api.md): Endpoints, parameters and error codes

## Optional

- [Changelog](https://acme.example/changelog.md): Release history

That follows the llms.txt format: an H1 name (the only required part), a blockquote summary, and H2 sections of Markdown links with optional notes. Linking to plain Markdown versions of your pages (.md) is what the proposal recommends, because they're easier for language models to read than full HTML. An "Optional" section marks links that a tool can skip when it needs a shorter context.

Before uploading it, check these points:

  • Point Sitemap: at an XML sitemap. The field is pre-filled with an llms.txt address, but search engines expect a sitemap in XML format there. Put your sitemap.xml URL in that field; crawlers will find llms.txt at its standard location anyway.
  • Add a User-agent: * group if you need one. The builder only writes rules for the eight named crawlers. Every other crawler, including Googlebot and Bingbot, gets no rules from this file, so your protected paths don't apply to them. If you already have a robots.txt, merge these groups into it rather than replacing it.
  • Decide about search crawlers separately. Everything defaults to Disallow, including PerplexityBot, which Perplexity uses for its search results rather than model training. The matrix doesn't include OpenAI's OAI-SearchBot or Anthropic's Claude-SearchBot. If you want to stay visible in AI search, add Allow groups for them yourself.
  • Google-Extended and Applebot-Extended are opt-out tokens, not crawlers. Disallowing them stops Google and Apple from using your content for AI training. It doesn't affect Google Search or Apple search results.

What robots.txt Can't Do

robots.txt is a public request, not access control. Compliant crawlers follow it; scrapers that don't care will ignore it, and anyone can read it, so never list secret paths in it. Protect private areas with authentication. To find crawlers that ignore your rules, combine robots.txt with server-side rate limits or a honeypot link.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does llms.txt help my Google rankings or AI Overviews?
No. Google says Google Search doesn't use llms.txt and that it won't positively or negatively affect visibility or rankings. Publishing one is harmless, but focus on normal SEO for Google.
Does llms.txt stop AI companies from training on my content?
No. llms.txt only lists pages you'd like AI tools to read. To opt out of training, disallow the training crawlers and tokens in robots.txt, such as GPTBot, ClaudeBot, CCBot, Google-Extended and Applebot-Extended.
Should the Sitemap line point to llms.txt?
No. The Sitemap directive in robots.txt is for XML sitemaps. Enter your sitemap.xml URL in that field. llms.txt should simply be served at /llms.txt on your domain.
Will blocking these crawlers remove my site from Google?
No. None of the eight crawlers in the matrix is Googlebot. Disallowing Google-Extended only opts your content out of Gemini training and grounding, and Google says it doesn't affect Search ranking.
Is anything I type sent to a server?
No. Both files are generated in your browser, and downloads are created locally. The tool doesn't fetch or publish anything on your site.
