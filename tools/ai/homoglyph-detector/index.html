⚠️ Detection Boundaries & Operational Security Notice

Client-side character analysis highlights script mixing and lookalike codepoints, but cannot verify domain registration status, TLS trust chains, or backend safety. Many legitimate internationalized domain names (IDNs) legitimately employ non-Latin scripts for native-language audiences. Conversely, brand-jacking and phishing campaigns also employ non-homoglyph techniques such as typosquatting, sub-domain padding, or combo-squatting that cannot be diagnosed through Unicode script inspection alone. Always pair homoglyph checks with authoritative WHOIS and DNS record evaluations. Understanding Homograph & Punycode Attacks

Internationalized Domain Names (IDNs) allow people around the world to use domain names in local languages and scripts. However, many Unicode characters from different scripts look identical or near-identical to standard ASCII characters:

  • Cyrillic Small Letter A (U+0430): Visually identical to Latin a (U+0061), but processed as an entirely different target by DNS resolvers.
  • Punycode Conversion (RFC 3492): Browsers convert internationalized labels into ASCII-compatible encoding prefixed by xn-- (e.g., аpple.com becomes xn--pple-43d.com).
  • Mixed-Script Traps: Phishers substitute one or two characters in popular domain names to craft fraudulent links that deceive visual inspection in emails, chat apps, and web links.
