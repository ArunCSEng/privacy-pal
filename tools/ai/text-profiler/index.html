⚠️ Stylometric Profiling & Detection Boundaries

Statistical text metrics and perplexity proxies cannot definitively determine human vs. synthetic authorship. While unedited LLM outputs often demonstrate uniform sentence cadences (low burstiness) and conservative vocabulary selections (lower Shannon entropy), highly technical human writing, legal contracts, and academic papers exhibit nearly identical repetition distributions. Never use stylometric heuristics as the sole evidence in academic or forensic investigations. How Text Perplexity & Repetition Profiling Operates

Large language models select tokens based on probabilistic distribution chains. This generation dynamic often produces characteristic statistical signatures:

  • Type-Token Ratio (TTR): Measures lexical diversity by calculating the proportion of unique words relative to total words. Repetitive phrasing or repetitive instruction adherence depresses this score.
  • Shannon Entropy: Measures information density per character. Predictable or constrained sentence structures yield lower entropy values.
  • Burstiness & Cadence: Human writers tend to vary sentence lengths dramatically (e.g., alternating between 3-word statements and 35-word clauses). Language models typically default to balanced, mid-length sentences, resulting in low variance.
  • N-Gram Collocation Clustering: Tracks multi-word sequences (bigrams and trigrams) to detect automated phrase loops, boilerplate phrasing, and loop-hallucinations.
