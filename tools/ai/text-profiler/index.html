What This Tool Measures

The text profiler calculates simple writing statistics: how varied the vocabulary is, how much sentence length changes, and which word pairs and triples repeat. These are useful for editing, such as spotting repetitive phrasing or monotonous rhythm. They're the same kinds of signals people mention when talking about AI-written text, but on their own they can't tell you who or what wrote something.

Metric How it's calculated What it tells you
TTR (Lexical Richness)Unique words ÷ total wordsVocabulary variety. It falls naturally as text gets longer, so only compare samples of similar length
Shannon EntropyBits per character, from how often each character appearsCharacter variety. Ordinary English prose almost always lands around 4.1 to 4.5, so it rarely separates one text from another
Burstiness ScoreStandard deviation of sentence length ÷ average sentence lengthHow much sentence length varies. Low values mean an even, uniform rhythm
Perplexity Proxy2 raised to the word-level entropy of the text's own word countsRoughly, how many "equally likely" words the text uses. It's not language-model perplexity, which needs a trained model
Top Bigrams / TrigramsAll 2- and 3-word sequences, sorted by countRepeated phrases. Entries showing 1x aren't repeated

Sentences are split at full stops, question marks and exclamation marks, so abbreviations and numbers such as "e.g.", "U.S." and "3.5" create extra short "sentences" and affect the burstiness score.

How the Banner Decides

  • "Low Burstiness & Uniform Cadence Detected" appears when burstiness is below 0.35, TTR is below 0.65 and the text has more than 40 words.
  • "High N-Gram Repetition Density" appears when the most common word pair makes up more than 8% of all words.
  • Otherwise it shows "Dynamic Cadence & Variable Word Distribution".

Because TTR drops as length grows, the same writing can pass when short and be flagged when long.

How to Use the Text Profiler

  1. Paste at least 30 characters of text and click "Analyze Profile". Longer passages give steadier numbers.
  2. Compare like with like. Profile drafts of similar length and genre, for example two versions of the same article.
  3. Use the repetition lists for editing. Phrases that repeat 3x or more in a short piece are often worth rewording.
  4. Export the metrics as JSON to track changes between drafts.

Why Statistics Can't Identify AI Text

  • Human writing can be uniform. Technical documentation, legal text, and writing by people working in a second language often have even sentence lengths and a limited vocabulary.
  • AI writing can be varied. Asking a model to vary its style, or lightly editing its output, changes these numbers easily.
  • Even dedicated detectors struggle. OpenAI withdrew its own AI text classifier in 2023 because of its low accuracy. A 2023 Stanford study found that popular detectors flagged more than half of essays by non-native English speakers as AI-generated.

Don't use these metrics, or any single detector, to accuse someone of using AI. Treat them as editing feedback.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can this tool tell whether text was written by AI?
No. It measures vocabulary variety, sentence-length variation and repeated phrases. Those differ between writers and genres, and the banner's thresholds depend heavily on text length. In our test, simply pasting the same paragraph twice changed the result.
What is burstiness?
Here it's the standard deviation of sentence length divided by the average sentence length. Writing that mixes short and long sentences scores higher; writing where every sentence is a similar length scores lower.
Is the Perplexity Proxy the same as an AI detector's perplexity?
No. Detector perplexity measures how predictable text is to a trained language model. This proxy only uses the word counts within the text you paste, so it mostly reflects how many different words you used.
Why does TTR go down for longer texts?
Common words such as "the", "and" and "of" keep repeating, while new words appear less often as a text grows. So the share of unique words falls with length. Compare TTR only between texts of similar length.
Is my text uploaded?
No. All calculations run in JavaScript in your browser, and the tool makes no network requests with your text. The exported JSON is created locally.
