⚠️ Forensic Caution & Algorithmic Boundaries

Visual artifact and gradient inspection is a forensic aid, not definitive verification of authenticity or synthetic fabrication. Face-swapping architectures and generative inpainting pipelines often leave slight luminance discontinuities, edge-feathering artifacts, or mismatched chroma channels along composite boundaries. However, standard photo editing, uneven lighting, flash photography, makeup, lens vignette, and aggressive multi-pass JPEG compression frequently trigger identical visual anomalies. Never rely solely on visual filters to assert deepfake presence; always correlate findings with cryptographically verifiable provenance records (C2PA) and authentic source camera metadata.

How Deepfake Artifact Inspection Works

Deepfake generators, face-swapping algorithms (e.g., DeepFaceLab, SimSwap, RoOP), and generative inpainting models splice a target face into an existing destination body or background. This process often introduces distinct mathematical anomalies:

  • Seam Gradient Derivation: Computes local Sobel edge differentials to expose artificial softening or blur halos where the synthetic face mask was blended onto the skin surface.
  • Gamma & Non-Linear Contrast Stretching: Exposes subtle tone mapping mismatches between the foreground subject and background lighting that are invisible to the naked human eye.
  • Chroma Mismatch Isolation: Isolates differences between color channels (Red vs. Blue/Green channels) to detect inconsistencies in color grading and white balance between donor and destination assets.
