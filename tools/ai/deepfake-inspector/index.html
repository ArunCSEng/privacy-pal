What This Tool Does

This is a visual inspection aid, not an automatic deepfake detector. It doesn't use a machine-learning model and it can't tell you whether an image is fake. It gives you three filters that make some editing traces easier to see, such as a pasted region with different texture, mismatched lighting, or an odd colour cast, so you can look for them yourself.

How to Use the Deepfake Visual Inspector

  1. Load an image. Use the highest-quality copy you can find. Every re-upload and resize smooths away the traces these filters show.
  2. Start with Seam Gradient at about 6×. Natural photos have fairly even grain and texture. Look for a patch, often a face, that's noticeably smoother, sharper or edged by a thin outline.
  3. Switch to Gamma Contrast and raise the slider to brighten shadows. Check that shadows fall the same way on every person and object.
  4. Try Chroma Mismatch to compare colour casts, for example a face that looks warmer or cooler than the neck and hands.
  5. Export the view with "Export Inspection Canvas" to keep a PNG of what you saw.

What the Numbers Mean

The two numbers and the warning banner are rough statistics, not a verdict. Here's what they actually measure:

  • Color Channel Disparity is the average difference between red and green, and between red and blue, sampled from every 8th pixel of a copy up to 600 pixels wide. It measures how colourful the image is. Despite the ΔE label, it isn't a CIE colour-difference value. A black-and-white photo scores 0; a strongly coloured one scores high.
  • Boundary Gradient Index is the average brightness of the filtered view, so it depends on the current mode and rises as you move the slider.
  • The warning banner appears when the disparity is above 34 or the index is above 65. A vivid but genuine photo, or any image at a high slider setting, can trigger it.

What These Filters Can't Catch

  • Fully AI-generated images. An image generated in one pass by a diffusion model has no pasted region and no seam. Look instead for content problems (hands, text, reflections, repeated patterns) and check provenance.
  • Heavily compressed copies. Social media and messaging apps resize and re-compress images, which evens out texture and hides seams.
  • Ordinary edits. Retouching, portrait-mode blur, beauty filters, HDR, noise reduction and sharpening all create texture differences that look like the warning signs.

For anything important, combine what you see here with other checks: look for C2PA Content Credentials, run a reverse image search to find earlier or larger copies, and confirm with the original source.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can this tool tell me whether an image is a deepfake?
No. It has no detection model. It applies three visual filters that can make pasted regions, lighting mismatches and colour differences easier to see, and you judge the result. The warning banner is based on simple averages and isn't reliable on its own.
Why does my real photo show "Elevated Boundary & Chroma Variance"?
The warning appears when the colour disparity is above 34 or the gradient index is above 65. Colourful photos often exceed the first, and raising the slider pushes any image over the second. It doesn't mean the photo was edited.
What should I look for in the Seam Gradient view?
Even texture across the whole image. A region that's much darker (smoother) or brighter (sharper) than similar areas around it, or a thin outline around a face or object, is worth a closer look, especially if it lines up with something in the original.
Does the Amplification Factor change the result?
Yes. It multiplies the edge and colour filters and sets the gamma curve, so it changes both the picture and the Boundary Gradient Index. Compare images at the same setting, and use it to make faint differences visible rather than as a score.
Is my image uploaded?
No. The image is decoded and filtered on a canvas in your browser, the SHA-256 is computed with Web Crypto, and the tool makes no network requests with your image.
