Mathematical Foundation: Polynomial Interpolation over GF(256)

Shamir's Secret Sharing Scheme (SSSS), introduced by Adi Shamir in 1979, is an information-theoretically secure threshold cryptography algorithm. It relies on the principle that k points uniquely define a polynomial of degree k - 1:

  • Polynomial Creation: To protect secret byte S, a random polynomial f(x) = a0 + a1*x + a2*x^2 + ... + a_(k-1)*x^(k-1) is constructed where a0 = S and coefficients a1, ..., a_(k-1) are chosen uniformly at random.
  • Share Allocation: Each participant receives an evaluation (x_i, f(x_i)) for distinct non-zero values x_i in GF(256).
  • Lagrange Reconstruction: Any subset of k shares can reconstruct f(0) = S via Lagrange basis polynomials: $$S = f(0) = \sum_{j=1}^{k} y_j \prod_{\substack{m=1 \\ m \neq j}}^{k} \frac{x_m}{x_j \oplus x_m}$$

Why Galois Field GF(256)?

Performing polynomial arithmetic over standard real or floating-point numbers leaks order-of-magnitude bounds and introduces rounding precision issues. Over the finite field GF(256) (defined by Rijndael's irreducible polynomial x^8 + x^4 + x^3 + x + 1), addition and subtraction correspond to bitwise XOR, and multiplication is closed within byte values 0 to 255. This guarantees perfect secrecy: any k - 1 shares are compatible with every possible secret byte with equal probability.
