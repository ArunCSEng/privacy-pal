What Shamir's Secret Sharing Does

Shamir's Secret Sharing splits a secret into n shares so that any k of them reconstruct it, but any k−1 reveal nothing at all. You pick the threshold: "3 of 5" means you hand out five shares, any three rebuild the secret, and any two are useless. That gives you both resilience — you can lose some shares and still recover — and security, since compromising fewer than the threshold tells an attacker nothing. This tool splits and recombines secrets entirely in your browser.

Why It's Mathematically Secure, Not Just "Hard"

The scheme is built on a simple fact about polynomials: it takes exactly k points to define a degree-(k−1) curve. The secret is hidden as the constant term of a random polynomial of that degree; each share is one point on the curve. With k points you can interpolate the curve and read off the secret; with k−1 points, every possible secret is still equally consistent with what you hold. That's information-theoretic security — fewer than k shares give zero information, not merely "computationally hard to crack." The arithmetic is done byte-by-byte in the GF(256) finite field (the same field AES uses), so any secret, text or binary, can be split.

Two Things the Shares Don't Carry

Worth understanding before you rely on it:

  • The threshold isn't stored in the shares. You have to remember k yourself. If you combine fewer than k shares, the tool can't know you're short — it interpolates what you gave it and returns a wrong secret (here, often an invalid-text error), not a "need more shares" message.
  • There's no integrity check. A corrupted, mistyped or deliberately forged share won't be detected; it simply produces a wrong secret. If tampering is a concern, store a separate checksum of the real secret (or of each share) so you can confirm a correct reconstruction.

How to Use the Tool

  1. Split: choose the threshold and total share count, enter the secret, and generate the shares.
  2. Distribute the shares to separate people or locations, and record the threshold separately — you'll need it to recombine.
  3. Combine: paste at least k shares (one per line) and reconstruct the secret.
  4. Verify the result matches what you expect; with no built-in integrity check, a known checksum is the way to be sure.

Using It Safely

  • Shares are secret material. Together, k of them are the secret, so store and transmit them as carefully as the secret itself and keep them apart.
  • Good for high-value keys — a master key, recovery seed or root credential split so no one person holds it alone. For those, generate and split on an offline, trusted device.
  • Choose k and n deliberately. A higher threshold resists collusion; more total shares add redundancy against loss. Balance the two for your situation.
  • Runs locally. Splitting and combining happen in your browser — nothing is uploaded — which is why it's safe for real secrets.

Frequently Asked Questions

What do the threshold and total share numbers mean?
Total (n) is how many shares you create; threshold (k) is how many are needed to reconstruct. "3 of 5" makes five shares, any three of which rebuild the secret while any two reveal nothing. Choose k for how much collusion you want to resist and n for redundancy against lost shares.
Is this more secure than encrypting the secret?
It's a different guarantee. With fewer than k shares, Shamir's scheme leaks zero information — mathematically, not just "hard to break." Encryption protects a secret behind a single key; secret sharing removes the single point of failure by requiring a quorum. They're often combined.
What if I combine fewer shares than the threshold?
You get a wrong result, not the secret — and the tool can't warn you, because the threshold isn't stored in the shares. Always remember k and bring at least that many. Keeping a checksum of the real secret lets you confirm a reconstruction is correct.
Will it detect a corrupted or fake share?
No. This is plain Shamir sharing with no authentication, so a bad share just yields a wrong secret rather than an error. If you need to detect tampering, store a separate hash of the secret, or use a scheme that adds integrity checks to each share.
Are my secret and shares sent anywhere?
No. All splitting and combining run in your browser with its cryptographic random generator, and nothing is uploaded. For a high-value secret, do it on an offline, trusted device and keep the shares physically separated.
