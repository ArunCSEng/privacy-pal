How Asymmetric RSA Cryptography Works

The Rivest-Shamir-Adleman (RSA) algorithm utilizes the computational hardness of factoring the product of two large prime numbers $p$ and $q$ to form modulus $n = p \times q$. The public exponent $e$ (typically $65537$) and private exponent $d$ satisfy the modular congruence:

$$e \cdot d \equiv 1 \pmod{\phi(n)}$$

where $\phi(n) = (p - 1)(q - 1)$ represents Euler's totient function. Messages encrypted with the public key can only be decrypted by the corresponding private exponent $d$.

Export Encoding Standards (PKCS#8 & SPKI)

  • SPKI (Subject Public Key Info): Standard defined under RFC 5280 specifying ASN.1 DER encodings for public keys, universally encapsulated within -----BEGIN PUBLIC KEY----- PEM envelopes.
  • PKCS#8 (Private-Key Information Syntax): Standard defined under RFC 5958 for storing private keys in a uniform syntax across algorithms, encapsulated within -----BEGIN PRIVATE KEY----- PEM envelopes.

Zero Network Egress Security Guarantee

Generating cryptographic keys on untrusted cloud services exposes master keying material to interceptors and server-side logs. BitDark executes all key generation entirely inside your browser's sandboxed thread via window.crypto.subtle.generateKey. No entropy buffers or key data are transmitted across any network boundary.
