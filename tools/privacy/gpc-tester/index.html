⚠️ Legal Enforcement & Signal Limitation Notice

Emitting privacy headers does not ensure compliance by third-party tracking infrastructure. While regulations such as CCPA/CPRA, Colorado Privacy Act (CPA), and European GDPR recognize opt-out preference signals like GPC as legally binding opt-out requests, adherence is enforced server-side. Rogue analytical networks, ad-tech aggregators, and browser-fingerprinting endpoints may ignore Sec-GPC or deprecated DNT: 1 headers unless strictly restrained by network-level content blockers or tracking prevention extensions.

Understanding GPC vs. DNT Standards

Modern browser privacy signaling consists of two primary mechanisms operating across HTTP headers and JavaScript runtime APIs:

  • Global Privacy Control (GPC): Standardized through the W3C, navigator.globalPrivacyControl evaluates to true when enabled. It commands websites not to sell or share personal browsing data under statutes like the California Consumer Privacy Act.
  • Do Not Track (DNT): The legacy predecessor via navigator.doNotTrack. Set to "1" when active. While widely deprecated by major standards bodies due to lack of binding legal consequence, it remains an informative signal for consumer intent.
