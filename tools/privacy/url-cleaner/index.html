⚠️ Limitations & Disclaimer

Cleaning a link changes what you pass on, not what has already been recorded. By the time a tracking parameter is in your address bar, the visit it describes has happened and been logged. Removing it stops you forwarding an identifier to everyone you share the link with, which is worth doing, and that is the whole of what it achieves. One caution: parameters are classified by name against a list of known tracking families, and a site can use a similar-looking name for something functional — a referral code attached to a discount, a session value, an attribution the recipient would want preserved. Check the classification table before sharing anything that matters, and test the cleaned link if the stakes are high. Nothing is transmitted and no request is made to the URL. What This Tool Does

Paste a URL and the tool splits it into its parts, examines each query parameter, and reports what each one is — recognised tracking identifier or something it does not recognise and leaves alone. The tracking parameters are removed and a cleaned link comes back.

Showing the classification rather than just the output is deliberate. A link that silently loses a parameter the recipient needed is worse than one you looked at for five seconds.

How to Use the Tool

  1. Paste the URL into Enter URL to Sanitize — typically one copied from an address bar after arriving from an email, a search result or a social post.
  2. Click Clean URL. The table fills with one row per parameter.
  3. Read the table before taking the output. Each row shows the parameter name, its value, a classification and the action taken, so you can see exactly what was dropped.
  4. Look at anything left in place. Unrecognised parameters are kept, and some of them may still be identifying — a site's own bespoke tracking does not appear on any public list.
  5. Click Copy Sanitized URL and share that version.
  6. Test the cleaned link if it is important — a product page with a variant selection, or anything where the parameters might carry real state.

Two Kinds of Tracking Parameter

utm parameters are descriptive campaign labels: source, medium, campaign, term, content. They say which newsletter or advert a visit came from, and they are not unique per person — everyone who clicks the same link in the same email carries the same values. They are analytics metadata, and removing them loses nothing for you.

Click identifiers are different and more consequential. A value such as fbclid or gclid is generated per click, so it ties your arrival at the destination to one specific impression and, through the ad platform's own records, to the profile that saw it. Forward that link and the identifier travels with it; now the destination associates every recipient with a click that was yours.

When Removal Is the Wrong Move

Not every parameter that looks disposable is. Referral and affiliate codes often determine whether someone receives credit or a discount, and stripping them from a link a friend sent can cost them money. Some applications carry state in the query string — a filter selection, a page position, a shared view configuration — that the link is useless without.

The classification table exists for exactly this. Removal is driven by a list of known advertising and analytics identifiers, and anything outside that list is kept, so unfamiliar parameters survive by default. When in doubt, open the cleaned link before you send it.

What Cleaning Does Not Undo

A tracking parameter in the address bar is a record of something that already happened. The ad platform logged the click as you left it, the destination logged the arrival, and any analytics or advertising scripts on the page did their own recording the moment it loaded. Removing the parameter afterwards changes none of that.

What it does change is propagation. A link shared with a click identifier intact carries your identifier to every person who opens it, which associates all of them with your click in the destination's records. That is the leak worth closing, and it is entirely within your control: clean the link before you paste it into a message, a document or a post.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which parameters are removed?
The documented tracking families: the full utm set used by analytics platforms, plus advertising click identifiers including fbclid, gclid, msclkid, dclid, yclid, twclid, ttclid, igshid, mc_eid, the HubSpot _hsenc and _hsmi pair, vero_id and wickedid. Each is listed in the table with its classification so you can see what was dropped and why.
Could removing a parameter break the link?
It can. Some sites use a parameter that looks like tracking to carry real state — a referral code tied to a discount, a session identifier, an affiliate attribution the recipient may actually want credited. The tool classifies each parameter in the table rather than silently discarding everything, so check the result before sharing anything important.
What is a click identifier?
A per-click value an ad platform appends when you follow an advert. Unlike utm parameters, which are descriptive labels, a click identifier is unique to one click and ties your arrival at the destination back to a specific impression and, through the platform's records, to a profile. That makes it the more identifying of the two kinds.
Does cleaning a link protect me while browsing?
No. It controls what you pass on when you share a link, which stops you forwarding a tracking identifier to everyone who receives it. Your own visit was already recorded by the site and whatever scripts it loads, and removing a parameter afterwards does not undo that.
Is the URL sent anywhere?
No. Parsing and rewriting happen in your browser. Nothing is transmitted and no request is made to the URL you enter.
