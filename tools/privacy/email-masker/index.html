⚠️ Limitations & Disclaimer

Masking reduces disclosure; it does not guarantee anonymity. Two limits matter in particular. First, the SHA-256 option is unsalted: because the space of real email addresses is enumerable, anyone with a list of candidate addresses can hash them and match yours in seconds. That is precisely how hashed emails work as cross-site identifiers in advertising. It is a pseudonym, not anonymisation. Second, partial masking leaves a small candidate space, and with a known domain and naming convention it often leaves one plausible answer. The tool also only finds addresses that look like addresses, so obfuscated or malformed ones pass through untouched — always read the output before you share it. Everything runs in your browser and nothing is transmitted. Results are provided as-is; BitDark.net is not liable for decisions made from them.

What This Tool Does

Paste text containing email addresses — a log extract, a support thread, a spreadsheet column, a prompt you are about to send somewhere — and the matched addresses are replaced in place by one of four forms. The rest of the text is left alone, so the output stays readable and you can see what changed.

The point of offering four styles is that redaction is a trade. Each one keeps a different amount of information for the reader and therefore discloses a different amount about the person.

How to Use the Tool

  1. Paste your text into the input area. Whole files, log sections and chat transcripts are fine; addresses are found wherever they appear.
  2. Choose a Masking Style. Partial keeps the first and last characters of the local part. Redact All Except Domain keeps only the domain. Complete Redaction replaces the whole address with a placeholder. SHA-256 Pseudo-Anonymize substitutes a hash — read the section below before choosing it.
  3. Set the Mask Character if the default clashes with your format. Some log parsers dislike asterisks; a different character avoids the fight.
  4. Click Mask Emails and the output populates.
  5. Read the output before you use it. This is the step people skip. Check that every address was caught, and that what remains does not identify someone anyway from the surrounding text.
  6. Copy Output to take the sanitised text, then Clear Workspace to remove both copies from the page.

Why a Hashed Email Is Not Anonymous

Hashing feels like anonymisation because the output is unreadable, but that intuition fails whenever the input space is small. Passwords are hashed with a salt and a deliberately slow function because people choose guessable passwords. Email addresses are worse: an attacker does not need to guess at all. They take a list of addresses they already hold, hash each one, and look yours up.

That is not a theoretical weakness — it is the operating principle of hashed-email identity matching across the ad industry. The same address always produces the same hash, which makes it perfect for joining records between parties, which is the opposite of anonymity. So use the hash option when you want a stable pseudonym for correlating records in a dataset you control, and never when you need an address to stop identifying someone. For that, Complete Redaction is the honest choice.

Choosing a Style for the Job

For a bug report or a prompt pasted into an AI assistant, Complete Redaction is usually right: the address is incidental and nobody needs it. For analysing a support queue by organisation, Redact All Except Domain keeps what the analysis needs and drops what it does not. Partial is appropriate when a person must recognise their own address in a list — a confirmation screen, say — and inappropriate when the people in the text have any reason to avoid being identified.

What Masking Does Not Cover

The matcher looks for things shaped like email addresses, so deliberately obfuscated forms — jane dot doe at example dot com, an address split across a line break, one written inside a URL parameter — are not addresses as far as a pattern is concerned and pass straight through. So do names, phone numbers, account identifiers and internal hostnames, none of which this tool claims to handle.

There is also the problem that redaction cannot solve on its own: context. A sanitised ticket that says the only administrator at a named company could not log in identifies that person regardless of whether their address survived. Masking is one step in reviewing text before you share it, not the whole review.

A Worked Example You Can Reproduce

Paste the same two lines four times and run each style in turn. jane.doe@example.com becomes a partially starred form that still reveals the domain, the initial and the final character; becomes a domain-only form that reveals the organisation alone; becomes a flat placeholder that reveals nothing; and becomes a 64-character hex digest. Hash the same address twice and note that the digest is identical both times — that stability is the entire reason it works as an identifier, and the entire reason it is not anonymisation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the SHA-256 option anonymisation?
No. The hash here is unsalted, and the set of possible email addresses is small enough to enumerate, so anyone holding a list of addresses can hash them all and match yours instantly. An unsalted hash of an email is a stable identifier, not an anonymous one — which is exactly why hashed email addresses are used for cross-site ad matching. Treat it as a pseudonym with a public lookup table, and never as redaction.
Which masking style should I pick?
It depends on what the reader must still be able to do. Complete Redaction removes everything and is right when the address is irrelevant. Redact All Except Domain keeps the organisation, which is usually enough for support tickets and log analysis. Partial keeps the first and last characters, which helps a recipient recognise their own address but narrows the candidate set considerably. More kept means more leaked.
Can a partially masked address be reversed?
Often, with context. Keeping the first and last letter plus the domain leaves a small candidate space, and if the domain is a company with a known naming convention it may leave one plausible answer. Partial masking is for readability, not for protecting the identity of someone who matters.
Does this replace proper data handling?
No. It helps you tidy text before pasting it into a ticket, a chat or an AI prompt. It is not a compliance control, it does not touch databases or files, and it cannot find addresses written in unusual formats. Redaction done once on a copy does nothing about the original.
Is any of this sent to a server?
No. Matching, masking and hashing all happen in your browser; SHA-256 uses the browser's own crypto implementation. Nothing is transmitted, and Clear Workspace removes everything from the page.
