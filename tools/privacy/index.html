Understanding Client-Side Privacy Auditing & Zero Egress

When users upload files or sensitive documents to conventional web-based sanitizers, their data is typically transmitted across public networks and cached in remote cloud databases. This creates severe exposure risks, including unintentional regulatory non-compliance and exposure to third-party data breaches.

Zero Network Egress

Every tool in this directory processes files, credentials, and image headers locally in volatile memory using HTML5 Canvas, Web Streams, and DataView APIs. Disconnecting your device from the internet will not interrupt tool availability.

Dynamic Tool Discovery

When new privacy utilities are published under /tools/privacy/, this hub synchronizes via manifest.json automatically, registering and rendering the new tools without manual code edits to the hub.

Ad Script Isolation

Third-party advertising scripts (including Google AdSense) execute strictly within sandboxed layout boundaries and have zero technical access to in-memory files, stripped EXIF tags, or redacted PII strings.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are personal photos, documents, or sanitized texts uploaded to any server?

No. All media byte manipulation, EXIF redaction, PII regex scrubbing, and canvas steganography analysis take place purely in your local browser sandbox memory (RAM). Disconnecting your internet connection will not stop these tools from executing.

How does BitDark's browser fingerprint audit differ from external testers?

Most online fingerprint scanners harvest your browser hashes to build centralized marketing databases. BitDark reads and visualizes your local WebGL, Canvas, and AudioContext entropy entirely in-memory without collecting, persisting, or transmitting the data anywhere.

How does the automatic catalog synchronization work?

The client makes an asynchronous check to /tools/privacy/manifest.json. When new tools are added to that JSON array, they are merged into your local view and cached in your browser's local storage automatically.
