⚠️ Limitations & Disclaimer

The entropy figures here are illustrative, not measured. Real entropy depends on how a value is distributed across a population, which requires a large dataset of real browsers; this page has no such dataset and does not pretend to. The weights come from published fingerprinting research and are useful for comparing which properties leak more than others — they are not a measurement of how unique you personally are. Two further limits: this reads properties through standard JavaScript APIs, so a site willing to work harder can read more; and a profile captured here reflects this browser, on this device, at this moment, and will shift with updates, a different window size or a different network. Everything runs locally and nothing is transmitted. Results are provided as-is; BitDark.net is not liable for decisions made from them.

What This Tool Does

Any page you visit can read a long list of facts about your browser and device without asking: screen dimensions, timezone, language list, processor count, device memory hint, installed graphics details, and how your system renders a canvas or processes audio. This audit reads that list and shows it to you, grouped and ranked by how much each item narrows the field.

The value is not any single row. It is seeing how ordinary the individual facts are and how specific the combination becomes.

How to Use the Tool

  1. Let the audit run. It collects properties on load; Re-Run Audit repeats the collection if you change something.
  2. Read the high-entropy rows first. Canvas, WebGL and audio results sit at the top for a reason — they depend on your specific hardware and driver stack, so they vary far more between people than a language setting does.
  3. Note the rows you could plausibly change and the many you cannot. Timezone and language follow where you are and who you are; GPU model follows the machine you bought.
  4. Click Export JSON Profile to save the result, or Copy JSON to put it on the clipboard.
  5. Compare across browsers. Run the audit in a second browser on the same machine and diff the two files. The properties that stay identical are the device talking, and those are the ones no browser setting hides.
  6. Re-run after a change — a resized window, a different display, a browser update — to see which parts of the profile are stable and which drift.

Why Harmless Facts Combine Into an Identifier

No single property here identifies anyone. Plenty of people share your timezone; plenty share your screen resolution; plenty have eight logical processors. The problem is that these facts are largely independent, so each one divides the remaining population again.

A property shared by one person in ten contributes a little over three bits. Combine eight such properties and you are nominally one in a hundred million — the arithmetic is crude, since real properties correlate, but the direction is right. This is why a defence that hides one property rarely helps much, and why the research consensus is that most browsers are distinguishable from most others.

Canvas, WebGL and Audio

These three rank highest because they measure the machine rather than the configuration. Rendering identical text and shapes to a canvas yields pixel data that differs with font rasterisation, anti-aliasing, GPU and driver, so hashing the output gives a stable per-device value. WebGL exposes the renderer string along with numeric capabilities. Audio fingerprinting processes a generated signal through the audio stack and hashes the floating-point output, which varies with the implementation underneath.

All three need no storage, survive clearing cookies, and work in private windows. That is exactly why they are used.

What Actually Helps

Blending in beats hiding. A browser that randomises or normalises these values so that many users look alike — and that ships that behaviour to everyone by default — is doing the thing that works, because a crowd of identical profiles cannot be told apart. A browser customised uniquely to resist fingerprinting often achieves the opposite: an unusual combination of defences is itself a distinguishing signal.

Script blocking removes most of what is read here at a significant usability cost. Mode-based protections that normalise screen metrics, limit canvas and audio readouts and standardise the font list are the practical middle ground, and they are most effective when you leave them at their defaults rather than tuning them.

A Worked Example You Can Reproduce

Export a profile, then open a private window and export another. Diff them: the two files will be nearly identical, because none of these properties is stored. That is the demonstration worth doing — private browsing defeats cookies and does nothing at all against this. Now export a third profile from a different browser on the same machine and diff again: user-agent, language handling and some rendering details change, while GPU, processor count and screen metrics stay put. What persists across browsers is the device, and that part no browser setting removes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are the entropy figures measured from real browsers?
No, and that matters. Measuring entropy properly means knowing how a value is distributed across a large population, which requires a dataset this page does not have. The numbers shown are illustrative weights based on published fingerprinting research — useful for ranking which properties leak more than others, not as a measurement of your own uniqueness.
Does blocking JavaScript stop fingerprinting?
It stops most of what is shown here, since nearly all of these properties are read through scripts. It also breaks a large share of the web. A more practical position is to reduce the distinctiveness of what you expose rather than to hide everything, because a browser configured in an unusual way to resist fingerprinting can end up more identifiable than a default one.
Why is canvas rendering identifying?
Because drawing the same shapes and text produces subtly different pixels on different systems. Font rasterisation, anti-aliasing, the GPU and its driver all influence the result, so hashing the output yields a value that is stable for you and different for most other people — without storing anything on your device.
Does private browsing prevent this?
No. Private windows discard cookies and history when you close them, which defeats storage-based tracking. Fingerprinting reads properties of the browser and device, and those are identical in a private window. That is the point of the technique: it needs nothing stored.
Is my profile sent anywhere?
No. Every property is read in your browser and displayed on this page. Export JSON Profile and Copy JSON write to a file or your clipboard locally. Nothing is transmitted.
