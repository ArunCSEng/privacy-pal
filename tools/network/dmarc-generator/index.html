What This Tool Does

DMARC (Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting and Conformance) is the DNS record that ties SPF and DKIM together: it tells receiving mail servers what to do when a message claiming to be from your domain fails authentication, and it asks them to send you reports about the mail they see. This tool builds that record for you. Choose a policy and options in the form and it assembles a valid DMARC TXT record, explains what each tag does, and lets you copy it — all in your browser, with nothing sent anywhere.

DMARC on Top of SPF and DKIM

SPF lists which servers may send mail for your domain; DKIM adds a cryptographic signature a receiver can verify. On their own, neither says what a receiver should do on failure, and neither checks that the authenticated domain matches the address a human sees. DMARC adds both: a policy for failing, unaligned mail, and alignment, which requires the SPF- or DKIM-validated domain to match your visible From domain. A message passes DMARC when it is authenticated and aligned by SPF or DKIM.

The Tags in a DMARC Record

A DMARC record is a list of tag=value pairs. This tool writes them in the correct order, beginning with v=DMARC1:

TagMeaning
vVersion; always DMARC1, and always first.
pPolicy for the domain: none, quarantine or reject.
spA separate policy for subdomains, if you want one.
pctPercentage of failing mail the policy applies to (1–100), for a gradual rollout.
ruaAddress(es) for daily aggregate (XML) reports.
rufAddress(es) for per-message failure reports.
adkim / aspfDKIM and SPF alignment mode: relaxed (r) or strict (s).
foWhen to generate failure reports.
riHow often (in seconds) you want aggregate reports.

A Worked Example You Can Reproduce

Choose p=none, set an aggregate address, and leave alignment relaxed. The tool produces a record like: 

v=DMARC1; p=none; pct=100; rua=mailto:dmarc@example.com; adkim=r; aspf=r;

You would publish this as a TXT record at _dmarc.example.com. It enforces nothing yet — p=none only monitors — but the rua address starts collecting aggregate reports so you can see every source sending as your domain. Once those reports show your legitimate senders passing and aligning, change p to quarantine and then reject, optionally using pct to ramp up. Each tag the tool adds is listed with a short description so you can see exactly what you are publishing.

How to Use the Tool

  1. Enter your domain and policy — start with p=none for monitoring.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is DMARC, and how does it relate to SPF and DKIM?
DMARC builds on SPF and DKIM. SPF checks which servers may send for your domain and DKIM signs messages; DMARC adds a policy that says what to do when a message fails those checks and does not align with your domain, plus reporting so you can see who is sending as you. All three are published as DNS records.
What does the policy (p) tag do?
The p tag sets what receivers should do with mail that fails DMARC: p=none only monitors and reports, p=quarantine asks receivers to treat failing mail as suspicious (usually spam-foldering it), and p=reject asks them to refuse it outright. Start at none to gather reports, then tighten once you have confirmed your legitimate senders pass.
What are the rua and ruf tags for?
rua is where receivers send daily XML aggregate reports — summaries of how much mail passed or failed, and from which sources — which are the main way to see your sending landscape. ruf is for per-message failure (forensic) reports, which are far less widely sent and can contain message data, so many operators set rua and leave ruf off.
What does alignment (adkim and aspf) mean?
Alignment requires that the domain SPF or DKIM validated matches the domain in the visible From address. Relaxed (the default) allows a subdomain to match the organisational domain; strict requires an exact match. adkim controls DKIM alignment and aspf controls SPF alignment. DMARC passes when the message is authenticated and aligned by at least one of the two.
Where do I publish the record, and is my input uploaded?
Publish it as a TXT record at _dmarc.yourdomain.com in your DNS. Nothing you type here is uploaded — the record is assembled in your browser, so you can build it with your real addresses and nothing is sent to a server. After publishing, you can confirm it with a DNS lookup of that name.
