RFC 7208 Sender Policy Framework Syntax Architecture

Sender Policy Framework (SPF) allows an administrative domain to specify which outbound hosts and IP subnets are authorized to send email on its behalf:

  • Qualifiers: Control the verdict returned when a sending host matches a mechanism. + (Pass - default), - (Fail/HardFail), ~ (SoftFail), and ? (Neutral).
  • IP Directives: ip4: and ip6: specify exact IPs or CIDR blocks. These authorize hosts directly and consume zero DNS queries.
  • DNS-Dependent Directives: a, mx, ptr, include:, and exists: trigger active DNS resolution.
  • 10-Lookup Limit: RFC 7208 §4.6.4 dictates that the total count of mechanisms and modifiers that trigger DNS lookups must not exceed 10 across the entire evaluation chain, including nested include: and redirect= directives. Exceeding 10 results in an immediate PermError verdict.
  • Deprecated Directives: The ptr mechanism is officially discouraged due to excessive reverse DNS lookups, reliability failures, and caching overhead.

Zero Network Egress Security Guarantee

SPF records often map internal mail infrastructure, third-party transactional gateways, and relay IPs. BitDark deconstructs and validates syntax entirely inside your browser execution thread. No hostnames, domains, or DNS strings are transmitted externally.
